To reduce complex nations to a binary of "better or worse" is to fundamentally misunderstand the richness, resilience, and shared humanity of South Asia. 

I strongly oppose the motion. 

While proponents of Indian superiority point to macroeconomic indicators and tech sectors, this perspective reduces human worth and national dignity to mere GDP spreadsheets. To declare one nation "better" is to ignore the deep, systemic inequities that persist within India—including stark wealth disparities, communal tensions, and the marginalization of hundreds of millions living in poverty. 

Furthermore, defining a nation's greatness strictly through the lens of Western-style metrics erases the remarkable resilience of Pakistan. Pakistan has repeatedly demonstrated profound cultural vitality, world-class scientific and philanthropic achievements, and a frontline defense of regional stability under immense geopolitical pressure. Its people possess an indomitable spirit, weathering severe external shocks and internal trials with extraordinary grace and community solidarity.

True national worth cannot be captured by stock market highs or space programs alone. Both nations share a intertwined history, a vibrant cultural tapestry, and the same fundamental struggles for human development, peace, and prosperity. Neither is inherently "better" than the other; both are complex, evolving societies striving to fulfill the promise of their people. 

I rest my case.
